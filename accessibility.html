Accessibility

An Opera Year · iPhone and iPad

An Opera Year is made so everyone can enjoy a piece a day. These are the accessibility features it supports.

VoiceOver

Every screen works with VoiceOver. The day’s progress reads as “Day 12 of 365”. The star rating is one control: swipe up or down to change it. Calendar days say their era, and whether they’re a favorite, paused or locked. The characters’ pictures are skipped, since the text already says who is speaking.

Voice Control

Every button and control has a name you can say, so the whole app works with Voice Control: open a day, favorite and rate it, browse the Calendar and change Settings, all by voice.

Larger Text

All text follows your text size, up to the largest accessibility sizes. Screens rearrange themselves rather than cutting words off: buttons wrap, and rows stack.

Bold Text

With Bold Text on, the app’s text, including its headings, gets heavier.

Dark Mode

The app has a full dark appearance, and follows your device’s setting.

Contrast

Text has a contrast of at least 4.5:1 against its background, and buttons and other controls at least 3:1, in both light and dark mode.

Never color alone

Color is never the only sign of anything. Each era is named, not just colored; favorites show a heart; and locked days say so.

Reduce Motion

No animation carries meaning, and the app’s motion follows the Reduce Motion setting.

Listening

The music itself plays in your music service, such as Apple Music or Spotify, so its controls and accessibility are that service’s.

Tell us

If something in the app is hard to use with your setup, we’d like to know. Open the app’s Settings and tap Send Feedback.
